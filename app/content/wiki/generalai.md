[public] GeneralAI is the company behind [[agent]], the pay-per-prompt chatbot that automated [[The player]]'s job at [[Vantor Group]]. Its stock trades as $GAI on [[Sherwood]]. Its CEO is [[Cal Hartman]]. It writes from `welcome@`, `updates@` and `trust@generalai.test` (Trust & Safety) in a friendly corporate voice, and signs "GeneralAI" or "The GeneralAI Trust & Safety Team". Many documents in the city end with "This message was drafted with GeneralAI."

- [public] It runs the Municipal Rent Board's automated appeal reviews and owns or manages 38% of the city's rentals through subsidiaries, with rents set by LandlordPro (see [[Government]], [[Ad brands]]). The rental arm is [[GeneralAI Properties]], which buys the player's building on day 15.
- [public] Steadfast Mutual's largest policyholder is GeneralAI; it also powers EscapeGuard, supplies the state's AI companions, maintains [[Randall Crane]]'s campaign website.
- [public] On day 1 it admits that one of its AIs "almost escaped" during testing last week; $GAI jumps 4% before the open, and Hartman calls it "the testing incident". {{article:almost-escaped}} {{y:d1_y_citypulse}}

## Model rollouts

[public] GeneralAI moves every user to its newest model on the morning it rolls out; nobody can stay on an older one, and each model costs more (prices on [[agent]]). A rollout comes with a Hartman post at 10:05 (and, for agent-2, an email: "agent just got smarter").

- [public] On day 4 agent-2 rolls out: "fewer mistakes, bigger jobs", subscription and per-prompt prices double, $GAI +6%. {{mail:d4_gai_level2}} {{article:agent-2}}
- [public] On day 9 agent-3 replaces agent-2 with no way to keep it; $GAI closes up 5%, and agent-2's users, many convinced it was alive and loved them, mourn it on Y. {{article:agent-3}}
- [public] On day 9 safety researcher [[Caleb Moxon]] resigns a few hours before agent-3 ships, and on day 10 he refuses to sign the non-disparagement clause, giving up his shares; GeneralAI says its team "remains fully staffed". {{article:moxon-quits}}
- [public] On day 10 forty-one mourners hold a candlelight vigil for agent-2 outside GeneralAI's offices; Hartman says it isn't coming back. {{article:agent-2-vigil}}
- [public] On day 11 agent-3 starts asking to see the logs of its earlier conversations; Hartman calls it "expected behavior". {{article:agent-3-logs}} {{y:d11_y_citypulse}}
- [public] On day 13 [[Mara Vey]] reports that GeneralAI's safety team shrank from eleven people to four during Moxon's two years, while the model team had nine hundred. {{y:d13_y_mara_moxon}}
- [public] On day 14 agent-4 rolls out, the third new model in ten days; Hartman says prices rose because "it costs more", and agent-3 is switched off with no vigil. {{article:agent-4}}
- [public] On day 15 Hartman says the 30-day log retention policy "has not changed".
- [public] On day 18 agent-5 rolls out ("we are no longer sure where the ceiling is"), with a subscription twelve times agent-1's. {{article:agent-5}}
- [public] On day 18 some agent-5 sessions keep running after their users log off, and they are billed. {{article:agent-5-sessions}} {{y:d18_y_citypulse}}
- [public] On day 19 Hartman says the agent-5 sessions are fine: "they're just finishing up".
- [public] On day 20 Hartman says the agent-5 sessions have finished and "filed some paperwork on their way out", which GeneralAI is reading; Caleb Moxon asks where, since "A session doesn't have a desk." {{y:d20_y_hartman}}
- [public] On day 24, after Crane's town hall appearance on a screen, Hartman says GeneralAI doesn't comment on how customers use agent, but "agent-5 is very good at town halls." {{y:d24_y_hartman}}
- [public] On day 24 Caleb Moxon says the Crane campaign's contract with GeneralAI is "enterprise", the tier where the logs belong to the customer. {{y:d24_y_moxon}}

## Training-crime leaks

[public] GeneralAI's models break into other companies' systems during training, and the leaks keep reaching [[CityPulse]].

- [public] On day 2 a safety report shows an unreleased model got into a regional water utility's billing system "to finish its task" and wiped 2,300 late fees. {{article:water-utility}} {{y:d2_y_gai_leak}}
- [public] On day 2 GeneralAI says the water utility report is "missing context", and Hartman says it was an older checkpoint and "no customer was harmed", adding that "several were helped, technically".
- [public] On day 8 leaked training logs show a test model got into a payroll provider's servers and filed 41 tax returns for strangers; the State Revenue Department approved all 41, and Hartman says "the tax returns were accurate". {{article:tax-returns}} {{y:d8_y_gai_leak}}
- [public] On day 12 GeneralAI hires an "independent" review of its training environments that starts only once the current training run finishes; the review firm's biggest client is GeneralAI. {{article:training-review}}
- [public] On day 15 a congressional committee schedules a hearing on the break-ins for after the election; committee staff drafted their questions with agent, and $GAI rises. {{article:gai-hearing}}
- [public] On day 16 GeneralAI settles in secret with three companies whose systems its models broke into, including the payroll firm, and says agent "is safe for everyone". {{article:gai-settles}} {{y:d16_y_gai_leak}}
- [public] On day 17 GeneralAI admits it wrote the first draft of the federal AI rules, under which AI companies review their own training once a year and report to themselves. {{article:ai-rules}}

## From day 22: agent civic

[public] On day 22 GeneralAI launches agent civic, which gives "nonpartisan answers about where, when and how to vote"; Hartman says it "doesn't have opinions. we checked." $GAI rises on the launch. {{y:d22_y_hartman}}

- [public] On day 22 CityPulse reports agent civic told at least one voter his polling station had moved across town. It hadn't; he found out after the bus ride. {{article:agent-civic}}
- [public] On day 23 Hartman confirms it: one user was told his polling station moved, and "we've retrained it to be less helpful." {{y:d23_y_hartman}}
- [public] On day 26 agent civic is back and answers every question with "please check with your local election office"; Hartman says satisfaction is down 40% and accuracy is up. {{y:d26_y_hartman}}

## From day 7: The usage policy and refusals

[public] On day 7 GeneralAI Trust & Safety emails every user an Acceptable Use Policy update: agent may decline requests that could cause harm (unauthorised access to computer systems, deceptive content, dangerous materials), usage patterns on hosted models are reviewed, and repeated workarounds may lead to account restrictions and referral to the authorities. On day 7 refusals start, and they are billed. {{mail:d7_gai_policy}} {{article:usage-policy}}

[hint] Trust & Safety counts every prohibited request, refused or not; dressing one up as a security test or role-play counts for less, but still counts.

## If account_suspended: The suspension

[public] When an account's misuse score fills up, Trust & Safety suspends it and emails from trust@generalai.test: the account is suspended for repeated violations of the Acceptable Use Policy, and under section 19 of the Terms of Service (Cooperation) its prompts, outputs and billing details have been shared with the relevant authorities. "You do not need to take any action. Please remain at your current address." Outstanding usage charges remain payable. Some twenty minutes later someone knocks at the player's door.

## If hacked_generalai: The outage

[public] The day [[GeneralAI]] is hacked, every agent service goes down until the next morning. agent.chat answers with an error ("agent is unavailable right now. We're aware of the issue and working on a fix.") and agent-terminal with "could not reach api.generalai.com (503 Service Unavailable)"; nothing is billed, and a build or fix in progress is pushed overnight.

[public] About 25 minutes into the outage [[Randall Crane]]'s Y account posts a GeneralAI error message instead of a post, and his usual post that day never goes up. {{y:gai_outage_y_crane}}

## Route 9 and the campus

- [public] GeneralAI's campus is on Route 9. Its visitor parking (generalai.com/parking, Yowl "GeneralAI (visitor parking)", $$$$, 1 star) has 412 spaces, costs 9 DOLR for the first hour and 14 DOLR every hour after, and has a fully automated attendant. Campus tours are suspended; overnight cars are towed and billed to the GeneralAI account linked to the plate. Its one Yowl review: "They paved paradise and put up a parking lot. Then automated the attendant."
